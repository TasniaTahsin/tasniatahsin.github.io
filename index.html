About

I am a doctoral researcher at Auburn University working at the intersection of MRI physics, image reconstruction, and data-driven analysis. I am especially interested in diffusion MRI and tensor imaging to better understand brain microstructure and neurological disorders.

Beyond MRI, I have prior work in antenna design and machine-learning pipelines for medical and biological imaging, giving me a broad perspective from hardware to algorithms.

Projects

AI for High-Accuracy Bacterial Detection, Segmentation, and Classification
Models: YOLOv11, U-Net (ResNet34 encoder), ResNet18

Built an end-to-end pipeline that detects bacteria in microscopy images, segments their shapes into instance-level masks, and classifies morphology for automated quantitative analysis.

Wearable Microstrip Patch Antenna for Health Monitoring
Tool: CST Studio Suite

Designed and simulated a ground-slotted rectangular microstrip patch antenna for wearable devices at 3.5–3.8 GHz, comparing flexible substrates such as Polyethylene, RT Duroid, Teflon, and PDMS to balance gain, return loss, and mechanical flexibility.

Data-Driven ADHD Diagnosis: Comparative Analysis Using PSD and SVM
Model: Support Vector Machine (SVM)

Developed a power spectral density (PSD)–based feature extraction pipeline on fMRI data with SVM classification, showing that carefully selected compact feature sets can outperform large feature spaces for ADHD vs. control discrimination.
